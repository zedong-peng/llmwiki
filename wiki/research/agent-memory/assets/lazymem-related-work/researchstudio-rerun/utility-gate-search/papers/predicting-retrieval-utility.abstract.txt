Title: Predicting Retrieval Utility and Answer Quality in Retrieval-Augmented Generation
Authors: Fangzheng Tian, Debasis Ganguly, Craig Macdonald
Venue: ECIR 2026, Advances in Information Retrieval, pages 368-385
DOI: 10.1007/978-3-032-21289-4_24
Evidence level: official Springer abstract/metadata only; full chapter is subscription-gated.

The paper defines retrieval performance prediction as estimating the utility of retrieved documents. Utility is explicitly quantified as the performance gain from generation with retrieved context over generation without context. It separately defines generation performance prediction for final-answer correctness and relevance. The proposed predictors combine retriever-centric query-performance signals, reader-centric perplexity of retrieved context conditioned on the query, and query-agnostic document quality/readability features in linear regression models. The abstract reports Natural Questions experiments and says combining feature categories gives the most accurate RAG-performance estimates.

This is a high-risk collision for any claim that predicting paired with/without-context answer gain is new. The abstract does not establish a cost-adjusted action policy, one-sided lower confidence certificate, persistent conversational-memory evaluation, or cross-corpus transfer. Those are the remaining possible deltas, subject to full-text confirmation.
